Hi all, I'm Rainer. As a student in the 90s I read about Tierra and was sure I'd build something like it one day. I finally did, and I'd love to show you: Evochora, a digital evolution research platform, open source (MIT).
Live demo, no install: https://evochora.org

An organism's code is its body, spread over the cells of a shared n-dimensional grid. In that same grid it harvests energy, sheds entropy and assembles its offspring with movable pointers. There's no external fitness function. Selection comes only from energy, entropy and space. Thermodynamic costs, energy sources and mutation operators are plugins, to make the physics of the world an experiment. Organisms are written in a spatial assembly language with a compiler, so behavior can be built from reusable modules. Runs are deterministic, recorded at configurable density (any stretch can be re-recorded in full), and the data opens in the usual tools (Python, R, DuckDB).

In test runs, I observed things emerge that I neither designed nor rewarded, and each swept through the population:
- one lineage evolved offspring dispersal, which loosened the kin structure of the population
- another switched the shell it builds around each child to a cheaper material
- a switch in the reproduction logic settled in opposite directions under different energy regimes
In each case I could trace it back to the exact changes in the code behind it (no knockout to confirm it yet).
Experiment records on Zenodo (data, build, config, video, analysis notebook), fully reproducible.

I don't come from academia and am not aiming for a career in it, but digital evolution has kept me at this for over a year. A paper up to this community's standards is still beyond me, but a JOSS submission is underway.

I'd welcome criticism, questions and suggestions. And I'm happy to help anyone who wants to try it out. Is there anything you'd like to test in a world like this?
