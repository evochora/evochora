EVOCHORA
Simulation Platform for Digital Evolution Research

Run, observe and analyze digital evolution

Evochora is an open-source platform for digital evolution research. Its organisms are self-replicating programs laid out in a shared spatial environment, where they live under thermodynamic constraints.

Everything they do is written in their code and therefore exposed to selection. In the published runs, evolution changed where offspring are built, what they are made of and when a parent reproduces.

For an experiment, the ancestors, their behaviors and the physics of the environment can be configured and extended. Its runs can be followed in a web-based visualizer and analyzer and analyzed further in Python or R. They are deterministic, so a change that spread can be traced back to the mutation behind it.

Why Evochora?

For an in-depth description of the physics, the first results, the research directions and the limitations, read the Scientific Overview.

Spatial organisms with programmable behaviorThe AncestorBehavior LibraryAssembly Specification

In Evochora, an organism is a program laid out in an n-dimensional grid. It can interact with its environment and other organisms. It searches its surroundings for energy, builds its offspring, decides where to place it and can take or execute cells of its neighbors. The system makes none of these decisions for it. They are behavior in its code and therefore exposed to selection.

The ancestors that come with Evochora harvest energy, hoard it and reproduce. Further behaviors can be written in EvoASM, Evochora's assembly language. Its compiler and modules let a behavior be written once and reused in different ancestors. A run can start from several ancestors, so that different strategies meet in one world.

Thermodynamic constraints and extensible physicsOwnership and ThermodynamicsPlugin InterfacesDynamic Environments

In Evochora, survival is thermodynamic, and organisms compete for energy and for space. Every instruction costs energy and produces entropy. Energy has to be harvested from the environment, and entropy can only be shed by writing into it. An organism dies when its energy runs out or its entropy exceeds a limit.

What an action costs, where energy enters the world, how offspring mutate and what becomes of the dead are plugins. Each can be configured or replaced. Barriers, gradients, seasons and catastrophes can be written as plugins too. The world itself becomes part of the experiment.

Deterministic and reproducibleReproducibilityReproduce a RunRecord a Stretch in Full

A run in Evochora is deterministic. The same version and the same configuration with its seed always give the same course, tick for tick, on Linux, macOS and Windows, with any number of processor cores, and regardless of whether the run was stopped and resumed.

Anyone can therefore produce the same data and check a result independently. The data match bit for bit, apart from the time of recording. This holds at any recording density. A run recorded at intervals, or any stretch of it, can be computed again and recorded at every tick. It then shows the same course in more detail.

Traceable from the population to a single instructionWhat a Run RecordsObserve a RunAnalyze the Data

At each recorded tick, Evochora stores the environment and every organism with its internal state. It also stores every death and every birth with its parent and its mutations. The analyzer turns these data into charts, and plugins can add further metrics. All of it is kept as Parquet files, an open format, for analysis in Python or R.

So when a lineage takes over a population, it can be followed back to the organism in which the change began. The stretch around that moment can then be recorded again at every tick. The visualizer shows the organism there instruction by instruction: its internal state, the machine code it executes and the surroundings it actually had.

Long runs and large populations on a workstationResourcesPublished ExperimentsThe Ancestor

On a workstation, Evochora executes several million instructions per second. Every organism executes one instruction per tick, so a population of a thousand organisms reaches several hundred million ticks a day. Published runs have reached billions of ticks.

How many generations that makes depends on the organisms. The default ancestor is deliberately large, a program of a few thousand cells with behavior routines for harvesting, hoarding and reproducing. With it, such a population completes on the order of a thousand generations a day. Simpler organisms can be expected to reach many times that.
