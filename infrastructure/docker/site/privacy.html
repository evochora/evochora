Privacy Policy

In short: this website sets no cookies, uses no tracking or analytics and stores nothing in your browser. To stay up to date, it loads the list of releases and the video at the top of the page from GitHub, and the list of published experiments with their video clips from Zenodo.

Hosting and server log files

This website runs on a server in the Oracle Cloud, in the region Germany Central (Frankfurt). When you visit it, the server processes the access data that are technically necessary to deliver it: IP address, date and time of the request, the requested file, the status code and the browser type.

These data may be written to log files to keep the website secure, for example to investigate abuse. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. Log files are stored for at most 7 days and then deleted, unless they are needed to investigate a specific incident. A data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR is in place with the hosting provider.

The live demo

The visualizer and the analyzer load the recorded simulation runs from this server and process them in your browser. They need no account and send nothing about you to the server beyond the requests themselves.

Releases and video from GitHub

The download section shows the latest release with its version and its archives, and the top of the page shows a video. For this, your browser retrieves data from the servers api.github.com and raw.githubusercontent.com. The provider is GitHub, Inc., 88 Colin P Kelly Jr Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. Your IP address is transmitted to GitHub in the process. No cookies are set, because the requests are made without credentials.

The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. The legitimate interest is to show the current release without duplicate maintenance. For the transfer to the USA there is an adequacy decision of the European Commission (EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework) under which GitHub is certified. More information: GitHub privacy statement.

Published experiments from Zenodo

The section on published experiments shows the records of the Evochora community on Zenodo, each with a video clip. For this, your browser retrieves data from the server zenodo.org. The provider is CERN, the European Organization for Nuclear Research, Esplanade des Particules 1, 1211 Geneva 23, Switzerland. Your IP address is transmitted to Zenodo in the process, and Zenodo counts the retrieval of a clip in the public statistics of the record.

The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. The legitimate interest is to show every published experiment as soon as it appears. More information: Zenodo policies.

External links

Links to GitHub, Zenodo, Discord and Google Colab lead to those services, which apply their own privacy rules. Nothing is transmitted to them before you follow a link.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access the data held about you, to have them corrected or deleted, to restrict their processing and to object to it. You can also lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority. To exercise these rights, write to the address given above.
